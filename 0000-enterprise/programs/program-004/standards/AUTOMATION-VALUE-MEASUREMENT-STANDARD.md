# Automation Value Measurement Standard

## Purpose
Require evidence that each automation produces measurable business value without creating unacceptable operational, control, customer, or workforce harm.

## Baseline
Before pilot approval, the business owner must document the current manual or existing-process baseline, including volume, cycle time, error rate, labor effort, cost, service level, customer impact, and control burden where applicable.

## Required Measures
Each automation must define:
- intended outcome and beneficiary
- leading and lagging indicators
- quality and control metrics
- time or capacity released
- avoided cost or incremental value
- customer and employee impact indicators
- risk-adjusted operating cost
- measurement source, owner, cadence, and confidence level

## Value Calculation Rules
Benefits must distinguish observed results from forecasts. Assumptions, exclusions, one-time implementation effort, recurring platform expense, support effort, exception handling, and failure costs must be visible.

## Guardrails
Efficiency gains do not justify bypassing approval, privacy, safety, quality, accessibility, security, or human-control requirements. Negative externalities must be recorded alongside benefits.

## Review Cadence
Value performance must be reviewed after pilot, after material change, and at least quarterly for active production services.

## Decision Outcomes
Reviews may result in continuation, optimization, scope reduction, additional controls, temporary suspension, or retirement.

## Evidence Rule
An automation may not be represented as successful solely because it executes. Success requires sustained outcome, quality, control, and cost evidence against the approved baseline.
