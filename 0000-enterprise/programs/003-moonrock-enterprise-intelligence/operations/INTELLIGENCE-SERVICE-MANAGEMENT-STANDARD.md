# Intelligence Service Management Standard

## Purpose
Establish minimum operational controls for any MEI capability offered as a managed internal service.

## Service Definition
Every service must have a documented owner, purpose, users, supported intelligence products, dependencies, data classifications, service hours, support path, and lifecycle state.

## Required Practices
- Service catalog registration and ownership
- Access provisioning and periodic review
- Availability, quality, latency, and cost monitoring
- Incident, problem, change, release, and configuration management
- Backup, recovery, continuity, and rollback testing
- Security, privacy, retention, and audit controls
- User support, feedback capture, and outcome measurement
- Capacity planning and continual improvement

## Operational Records
Maintain service decisions, changes, releases, incidents, exceptions, quality results, access reviews, recovery tests, and improvement actions in approved repositories or registers.

## Severity Model
- Critical: security, privacy, integrity, or business-impacting failure requiring immediate suspension or containment.
- High: major degradation or materially unreliable intelligence.
- Moderate: limited degradation with an available workaround.
- Low: minor defect, request, or documentation issue.

## Change Control
Material changes to sources, models, schemas, confidence methods, delivery audiences, or approval rules require impact assessment, testing, approval, release documentation, and rollback capability.

## Retirement
Services must be retired through an approved plan covering user notice, dependency removal, record retention, access revocation, archival, and final ownership acceptance.
