# Forecasting and Trend Governance

Forecasts must identify the forecast horizon, baseline date, method, assumptions, scenarios, data period, confidence range, owner, and review cadence.

Forecast accuracy must be measured against actual outcomes and used to improve future methods. Material model or assumption changes require versioning and comparability disclosure.

Trend analysis must account for seasonality, structural changes, data gaps, one-time events, and changes in definitions. Short-term movement must not be represented as a durable trend without adequate evidence.

Forecasts are decision inputs, not guarantees. High-impact actions require human review of uncertainty, downside exposure, and alternative scenarios.

Nova may generate projections and explain drivers but must disclose assumptions and may not independently commit resources based on forecast output.
