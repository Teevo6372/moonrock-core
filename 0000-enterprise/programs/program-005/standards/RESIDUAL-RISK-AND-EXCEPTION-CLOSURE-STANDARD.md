# Residual Risk and Exception Closure Standard

## Purpose
Define how unresolved risks, defects, exceptions, and follow-up obligations are evaluated and dispositioned before Program 005 closes.

## Required Register
Maintain a closure register containing:
- item identifier and description;
- affected component, control, and owner;
- severity, likelihood, impact, and residual risk;
- current status and supporting evidence;
- compensating controls;
- due date, review date, and expiration date;
- decision authority and final disposition.

## Permitted Dispositions
An item may be:
- resolved and verified;
- transferred to an accountable operational backlog;
- accepted for a defined period by authorized leadership;
- deferred with approved scope restriction and compensating controls;
- rejected, requiring pilot suspension or closure without advancement.

## Closure Rules
- Critical unresolved risks may not be administratively closed.
- High-severity items require explicit authorized disposition.
- Exceptions must have an owner and expiration date.
- Transfer to another backlog does not eliminate accountability.
- Stale, ownerless, or unverifiable items block clean program closure.

## Risk Acceptance
Acceptance records must identify the affected scope, evidence reviewed, business rationale, residual exposure, monitoring, compensating controls, approver, and expiration or revalidation trigger.

## Exception Expiration
Expired exceptions revert to noncompliant status unless renewed through documented review. Renewal requires current evidence and may not be automatic.

## Verification
Resolved items require evidence that the corrective action operates as intended. Documentation changes alone are insufficient when operational validation is reasonably required.

## Reporting
The closure summary must distinguish resolved, transferred, accepted, deferred, and blocking items and must identify all future owners and deadlines.

## Prohibited Closure Practices
Items may not be closed by deleting records, relabeling severity without rationale, relying on implied acceptance, or assigning responsibility to an autonomous system.
