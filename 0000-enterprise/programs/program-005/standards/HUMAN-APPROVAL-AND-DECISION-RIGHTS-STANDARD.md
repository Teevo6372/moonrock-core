# Human Approval and Decision Rights Standard

## Purpose
Define who may approve, pause, change, expand, or terminate Program 005 pilots and ensure consequential decisions remain under accountable human control.

## Principles
- Authority must be explicit, documented, and limited to role and scope.
- Approval must be informed by current evidence and known risks.
- No system, agent, or automation may self-approve promotion or scope expansion.
- Conflicts of interest and incompatible duties must be identified and managed.

## Required Roles
Each pilot must identify:
- executive sponsor;
- service owner;
- implementation owner;
- operations owner;
- security and privacy reviewers;
- release or pilot approver;
- incident commander or escalation authority;
- rollback and termination authority.

## Decision Rights
Document who may:
- approve pilot initiation;
- accept residual risk or exceptions;
- authorize bounded scope changes;
- suspend or terminate the pilot;
- approve restart after a stop condition;
- approve pilot completion;
- recommend future production planning.

## Approval Requirements
Approvals must identify the candidate version, scope, evidence reviewed, unresolved risks, conditions, expiration, and approver. Silence, implied consent, or automated status changes do not constitute approval.

## Separation of Duties
Where practical, the individual who implements the candidate should not be the sole approver. Higher-risk pilots require independent security, privacy, operational, or executive review.

## Consequential Actions
Financial commitments, legal representations, employment decisions, regulatory submissions, destructive changes, irreversible actions, and material customer-impacting decisions require explicit authorized human review and action outside autonomous workflows.

## Emergency Authority
Named personnel may pause, isolate, roll back, or terminate a pilot without prior committee approval when required to protect people, data, customers, systems, or Moonrock. Emergency actions must be documented and reviewed afterward.

## Records
Retain role assignments, approvals, recusals, conditions, decision timestamps, evidence references, emergency actions, and final disposition.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented rationale, temporary decision authority, compensating oversight, approval, and expiration. Decision rights may not be delegated to an autonomous system.
