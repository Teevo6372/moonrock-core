# Capability Prioritization and Activation Standard

## Purpose
Provide a consistent method for comparing capability investments while preserving executive judgment and readiness controls.

## Priority Classes
- P0 Foundational — required for safe or consistent operation of dependent capabilities.
- P1 Immediate Revenue — supports near-term sales, cash flow, client acquisition, or product revenue.
- P2 Operational Efficiency — reduces cost, time, error, rework, or executive workload.
- P3 Expansion — enables scale, new markets, new divisions, or broader delivery.
- P4 Future Innovation — strategically relevant but not ready for near-term investment.

## Scoring Factors
Each proposed or developing capability should be assessed for:
- Revenue potential
- Time to value
- Customer value
- Strategic importance
- Dependency value
- Operational impact
- Current readiness
- Cost to activate
- Executive effort
- Risk and compliance exposure
- Automation potential
- Nova enablement
- Evidence quality

Scores support comparison; they do not replace owner judgment. Any override must state the reason, approver, and effective date.

## Activation Stages
1. Register the capability.
2. Assign owner, lifecycle status, maturity, and priority.
3. Define workflow, inputs, outputs, systems, approvals, risks, and metrics.
4. Complete dependency and current-stack assessment.
5. Complete Nova, automation, revenue, legal, or data gates that apply.
6. Obtain implementation-ready approval.
7. Run a controlled pilot with limits and rollback criteria.
8. Review pilot evidence.
9. Obtain active-status approval.
10. Monitor, optimize, restrict, pause, or retire based on evidence.

## Current-Stack Requirement
Before adding a platform or subscription, the capability owner must document:
- Existing tools that may satisfy the need
- Functional and governance gaps
- Integration and migration implications
- Incremental cost
- Data ownership and portability
- Exit strategy

## Activation Decision Record
Each activation decision must include capability ID, requested status, evidence, unresolved risks, conditions, approver, decision date, effective date, and next review.

## Suspension
A capability must be paused or restricted when material control failure, legal uncertainty, data exposure, repeated quality failure, unsupported cost, or client harm is identified. Restoration requires documented remediation and approval.
