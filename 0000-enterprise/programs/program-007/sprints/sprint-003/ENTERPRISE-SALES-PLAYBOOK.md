# Enterprise Sales Playbook

## Purpose
Provide a reusable, consultative sales method for Moonrock advisors while preserving the authority, evidence, and client-care rules established by MBOS.

## Sales Philosophy
Moonrock does not begin with a product pitch. Advisors first understand the business problem, desired outcome, constraints, and evidence. The recommended solution may involve one service, multiple divisions, an external referral, a phased plan, or no immediate sale.

## Advisor Preparation
Before a meeting, review the lead source, available business information, prior interactions, likely stakeholders, stated need, relevant division capabilities, unresolved questions, and potential conflicts. Do not assume budget, authority, urgency, or fit.

## Discovery Conversation Structure
1. Establish purpose and permission.
2. Invite the prospect’s story and desired outcome.
3. Clarify the current process and business impact.
4. Explore prior attempts and existing tools.
5. Quantify cost, risk, delay, or missed opportunity where evidence permits.
6. Confirm stakeholders, authority, budget path, and timeline.
7. Reflect the problem back in plain language.
8. Agree on the appropriate next step.

## Consultative Question Areas
Business model, customers, offers, revenue, lead flow, conversion, fulfillment, staffing, technology, data, documentation, customer experience, reporting, financial constraints, risk, continuity, and growth priorities.

## Recommendation Method
A recommendation should connect:
Problem → Evidence → Business impact → Proposed outcome → Recommended action → Dependencies → Investment → Risk → Success measure.

## Objection Handling
Treat objections as information, not resistance to overcome. Clarify the underlying concern, verify understanding, provide evidence, acknowledge tradeoffs, and offer an honest next step. Do not use false urgency, hidden scarcity, guilt, unsupported comparisons, or pressure.

## Common Objection Paths
- **Price:** clarify value, scope, alternatives, phasing, and cost of inaction without inventing savings.
- **Timing:** identify dependencies and determine whether nurture or phased work is appropriate.
- **Trust:** provide process transparency, evidence, references where authorized, and clear boundaries.
- **Existing provider/tool:** perform the current-stack and overlap review before recommending replacement.
- **Authority:** involve the appropriate decision maker rather than pushing an unauthorized contact.

## Follow-Up Cadence
Cadence must reflect consent, urgency, relationship stage, and channel rules. Each follow-up should add value, clarify a decision, deliver promised information, or establish closure. Repetitive pressure messages are prohibited.

## Proposal Conversation
Present the business problem and desired outcome before features. Explain scope, exclusions, responsibilities, timeline, investment, risks, and acceptance. Confirm understanding and document requested revisions.

## Cross-Division Selling
Discuss another division only when a supported client need exists. Explain the connection, preserve the Relationship Owner, and obtain permission before a handoff. Never bundle unrelated services merely to increase transaction size.

## Closing Standard
The goal is a clear, informed decision: proceed, revise, defer, nurture, refer, or decline. Silence, ambiguity, or a draft document is not acceptance.

## Post-Sale Handoff
Transfer discovery evidence, commitments, exclusions, stakeholders, risks, dependencies, payment status, and success measures into onboarding. Sales promises not reflected in the signed scope must be corrected before activation.

## Coaching and Review
Advisors should review discovery quality, conversion, proposal accuracy, client feedback, lost-opportunity reasons, cross-division value, and avoidable corrections. Coaching must reward trust, fit, documentation, and client outcomes—not revenue alone.
