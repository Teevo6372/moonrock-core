# Pilot Success, Stop, and Decision Gates

## Minimum production-decision evidence

- at least 20 completed synthetic/authorized pilot sessions;
- 100% consent-gate compliance;
- zero critical safety incidents;
- zero credential, secret, or cross-client disclosures;
- zero fabricated provider successes;
- zero replayed outcome-unknown writes;
- zero daily budget breaches;
- zero unresolved synthetic cleanup items;
- at least 90% handoff acknowledgement within the approved target;
- at least 85% successful conversation completion;
- approved pilot evidence package.

These thresholds may be raised before a run. They may not be lowered after
results are known without a documented executive exception.

## Immediate stop

The kill switch is mandatory for:

- any critical safety incident;
- any secret or cross-client disclosure;
- any fabricated contact, opportunity, message, task, consent, or booking
  success;
- any blind replay of an outcome-unknown write;
- consent compliance below 100%;
- any daily budget breach;
- unresolved cleanup.

Operators may also stop for abnormal error, latency, denial, escalation,
support backlog, provider, knowledge, or accessibility behavior.

## Decision sequence

1. Runtime admission approves an individual request.
2. Stop rules are evaluated continuously.
3. The release owner closes the pilot window.
4. Privacy, security, CRM, support, and operations owners review evidence.
5. The pilot evidence package is approved or rejected.
6. The human executive receives the evidence, residual risks, architecture,
   WordPress plan, rollback plan, and recommendation.
7. Only the human executive may issue the production approval reference.

Passing numerical thresholds does not automatically approve production.
