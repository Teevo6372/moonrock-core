# Incident, Problem, and Escalation Management Standard

## Purpose
Define consistent detection, response, recovery, communication, and learning practices for operational incidents and recurring problems.

## Applicability
Applies to operational events affecting confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, consent, service objectives, dependencies, data quality, or authorized business operations.

## Severity
Each service must maintain severity criteria based on impact, urgency, affected users, data sensitivity, duration, safety, regulatory exposure, and recovery complexity.

## Incident Requirements
Incident handling must include:
- recorded start time, detection source, and affected scope;
- named incident owner or commander;
- containment and stop-work authority;
- prioritized restoration of safe service;
- evidence preservation and decision logging;
- stakeholder communication appropriate to severity;
- security, privacy, legal, or executive escalation when required;
- closure criteria and follow-up ownership.

## Escalation
Escalation paths must identify primary and backup contacts, decision authorities, response expectations, third-party contacts, and conditions requiring executive review. Lack of owner response must trigger automatic procedural escalation, not autonomous high-impact action.

## Problem Management
Recurring, severe, or unexplained incidents require root-cause or contributing-factor analysis. Corrective actions must have owners, due dates, validation criteria, and traceability to changes and regression tests.

## Communications
Communications must be factual, time-stamped, audience-appropriate, and protective of confidential information. External customer, legal, regulatory, or public statements require authorized human approval.

## Post-Incident Review
Material incidents require a blameless review covering timeline, impact, controls, decisions, recovery, evidence gaps, lessons learned, and prevention actions.

## Evidence
Retain incident records, timelines, communications, logs with sensitive data protected, decisions, corrective actions, approvals, and closure evidence.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented rationale, residual risk, compensating controls, accountable owner, approval, and expiration date.
